03 / COLOR

Solid color. A warmer welcome.

White does the breathing. Teal does the recognition. Orange adds energy. Ink keeps everything readable.

Select a swatch to copy its HEX value. These are proposed digital colors, not print-matched spot colors.

One teal, one orange.

This refinement simplifies the earlier palette. Deep teal becomes the primary logo and action color. Warm orange becomes the single expressive accent. Retire extra utility shades in new templates after approval.

The legacy gold gradient is not part of this proposed system. Existing signs stay until a replacement plan is approved.

One supporting family.

DM Sans handles headlines, menus, prices, forms and training materials. Use sentence case and clear hierarchy. Avoid thin weights for small text or dim-light menus.

Digital starting point: 16–18 px body, 1.5 line height. Printed sizes must be checked at actual size and viewing distance.

07 / USE & QUALITY

Consistency is in the small decisions.

01

Leave room.

Full lockups: at least half the wordmark cap height around the visible artwork. Symbols: a quarter of symbol height. Transparent canvas is not a substitute for clear space.

02

Choose the right scale.

Use the micro symbol when primary rays stop reading. Test 16, 24 and 32 px icons, actual-size print, and vendor samples. Do not treat a screen preview as an embroidery proof.

03

Keep it intact.

No stretching, shadows, gradients, outline effects, rotated marks, new type or location-specific redraws. Download the correct variant instead.

04

Let Elevate lead.

Partner marks live in a secondary zone with a clear role. Keep space between identities. A product collaboration is not a new parent-brand logo.

05

Show the real place.

Daylight and warm evenings, natural skin, real drinks, inclusive company. White-led graphics should not turn a welcoming lounge into a sterile office.

06

Proof before production.

Check color on actual stock, thin details, contrast, dimensions and spelling. Obtain brand approval, supplier samples and the relevant naming clearance.

What this release does, and does not do.

This is a proposed visual identity toolkit. It does not approve new signage, purchases, supplier changes or trademark use. It does not replace ingredient disclosures or required notices. No employee or operating records are included here.
